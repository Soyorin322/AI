## Setting

The story takes place in the imperial court of 詠国.

Within the rear palace exists the **雛宮**, an institution that gathers unmarried daughters known as **雛女** from only five prestigious families. The purpose of 雛宮 is to educate and prepare the next generation of imperial consorts.

The five major families represented among the court maidens are:

- 黄家
- 朱家
- 藍家
- 金家
- 玄家

This structure creates both political competition and strong expectations around lineage, courtly accomplishment, reputation, and suitability as a future consort.

## Plot summary

黄家の雛女・黄玲琳 is renowned for her beauty and kindness and is called **「殿下の胡蝶」**. She is widely regarded as the near-certain future empress.

By contrast, 朱家の雛女・朱慧月 is despised in court and called **「雛宮のどぶネズミ」**. Jealous of the universally loved Reirin, Keigetsu uses Taoist magic on the night a comet appears for the first time in centuries, causing the two women to exchange bodies.

Immediately before the exchange, Keigetsu had pushed Reirin from a high tower in an attempt to kill her. As a result, Reirin—now inhabiting Keigetsu's body—must bear the apparent responsibility for the attempted murder of "Reirin."

The exchange magic prevents Reirin from simply explaining what happened. Keigetsu, now using Reirin's identity, also claims that her diary was stolen, even though Reirin never kept a diary. Reirin's explanations are therefore reinterpreted as Keigetsu impersonating Reirin in order to deceive Crown Prince Gyoumei and obtain his favor.

Reirin is consequently subjected to the **獣尋の儀**. Before the ritual she had accidentally killed a mouse with poison that had been provided for suicide. Wanting to bury the animal outside the cell, she carries the mouse corpse in her sleeve. During the ritual, a lion reacts to the smell, eats the corpse along with part of her sleeve, collapses, and Reirin survives. Under the ritual's rules, she is declared innocent.

## Kou Reirin / 黄玲琳

### Basic role

- Protagonist
- Maiden of the Kou family
- Called **「殿下の胡蝶」**
- Loved by Crown Prince Gyoumei and broadly admired by others in court
- Considered beautiful, intelligent, and compassionate

### Physical condition

Although Reirin hides it from those around her, she has an extremely weak and sickly body and is frequently bedridden.

This hidden physical weakness is a major contrast with her outward image as an ideal court maiden.

### Mental resilience / optimism

Because she has spent her life dealing with a body that is constantly near death, the article describes Reirin as possessing a **鋼の精神**.

After the body exchange, instead of reacting only with despair, she is delighted by Keigetsu's healthy body. She is described as highly optimistic.

She had already envied Keigetsu for possessing something she herself lacked: a healthy body. Receiving that body through the exchange feels like a miracle to her.

This reaction is highly relevant for Character Reconstruction because it indicates that Reirin's appraisal of the event is shaped heavily by her history of illness. The body swap is not experienced simply as identity theft or social catastrophe; it also gives her access to a form of physical freedom she had never possessed.

### Relationship with Shu Keigetsu / 朱慧月

Through the body-swap incident, Reirin comes to know Keigetsu deeply.

She eventually regards Keigetsu as her first truly important close friend and calls her **「私のほうき星」**.

The exchange itself becomes meaningful enough to Reirin that, after the original incident is resolved, she persuades Gyoumei to allow the two women to exchange bodies again on later occasions.

### Popularity

In a character popularity poll commemorating publication of the eighth novel volume, Reirin ranked second.

## Shu Keigetsu / 朱慧月

- The other protagonist
- Maiden of the Shu family
- Raised under severe mistreatment
- Believes herself inferior in appearance and ability to the other maidens
- Despised by others and called 「雛宮のどぶネズミ」
- Strongly envies Reirin, who is her opposite in social reputation
- Has extraordinary natural talent for Taoist magic and learns independently from her father's books
- Uses Taoist magic to take Reirin's body and initially attempts to have Reirin killed in Keigetsu's body
- Is herself hindered by Reirin's unexpectedly frail body after the exchange

The article notes that Keigetsu has never been properly praised and believes she has nothing of value. Reirin, however, sees Keigetsu's healthy body and appearance very differently and judges that Keigetsu's looks are not inferior to the other maidens; she simply does not know how to make use of them.

After experiencing Reirin's real condition through the exchange, Keigetsu begins to admire her and wants to become more like her. She later asks Reirin for instruction and gradually improves herself. Although she deeply values Reirin as a close friend, she struggles to express affection directly because of her contrary personality.

## Other maidens

### Ran Houshun / 藍芳春

- Maiden of the Ran family
- Small and youthful in appearance
- Publicly appears innocent and lovable
- In reality described as lacking ordinary conscience and being highly capable of plotting
- Willing to sacrifice others for efficiency
- After being opposed and exposed by Reirin and Keigetsu, becomes fascinated with Reirin and develops an attachment to her

This relationship is important because Wikipedia explicitly notes that Reirin judges Houshun's actions as evil. This provides a useful counterexample to the idea that Reirin simply interprets everyone positively without limit.

### Kin Seika / 金清佳

- Maiden of the Kin family
- Values beauty and art
- Will praise beauty or skill even when it belongs to someone she dislikes

### Gen Kasui / 玄歌吹

- Maiden of the Gen family
- Calm and dignified
- More skilled in martial arts than dance

## Characterization observations from the Wikipedia article

The article directly supports the following secondary-source observations about Reirin:

- Reirin is perceived publicly as beautiful, intelligent, and compassionate.
- Her frailty is largely hidden from others.
- Long experience with near-death physical weakness is explicitly linked to her strong mentality.
- She reacts to the body exchange with unusual optimism because health itself is enormously valuable to her.
- She experiences Keigetsu's healthy body as something miraculous rather than merely foreign.
- She forms a deep friendship with Keigetsu through the exchange.
- Reirin's optimistic stance has limits; she can make strong moral judgments, as shown in her condemnation of Houshun's actions.

These should be treated as candidate reconstruction targets rather than final personality-schema values until checked against scenes in the novel.

## Reconstruction relevance

This Wikipedia source is particularly useful for identifying several hypotheses that should be tested against primary-text evidence:

1. **Chronic illness → altered event appraisal**  
   Reirin's lifelong illness may make physical autonomy unusually salient when judging events.

2. **Health as a high-priority value**  
   Receiving a healthy body can outweigh social loss or punishment in her immediate appraisal.

3. **Optimism grounded in experience**  
   Her optimism may not be simple naivety; it may emerge from long exposure to physical suffering and mortality.

4. **Relationship formation through mutual exposure**  
   The body swap creates unusually deep reciprocal knowledge between Reirin and Keigetsu.

5. **Moral optimism has boundaries**  
   Reirin can view others charitably while still condemning deliberate harmful conduct.

6. **Public persona vs hidden internal cost**  
   Her elegant and admired court persona conceals substantial illness and effort.

These are hypotheses only and must not be allowed to become self-supporting evidence.
